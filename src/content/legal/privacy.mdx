## Information We Collect {#information-we-collect}

### Information You Provide to Us

We collect information you voluntarily provide, including:

- **Newsletter Subscriptions:** first name and email address, submitted through our newsletter form and processed by <a href="https://buttondown.com" rel="noopener">Buttondown</a>. When you submit the form, your IP address is also forwarded (server-side, not from your browser) to Buttondown as part of the subscription payload, which they may use for their own spam prevention and audit logs. If you reached the page through a campaign link, the `utm_source` and `utm_campaign` values from that link's address are recorded with your subscription so we can tell which of our announcements reached you. These are read from the address of the page you subscribe from and are never stored on your device; when the address carries no such values, nothing is recorded.
- **Survey Responses:** feedback provided through optional surveys we link to. Our surveys are hosted on Google Forms; responses are collected by Google on our behalf under their privacy terms.
- **Email Correspondence:** any information you include when emailing us directly (for example, at [hello@legesher.com](mailto:hello@legesher.com) or [privacy@legesher.com](mailto:privacy@legesher.com)).

Our own account record holds the identifiers and choices described under [Legesher Account](#legesher-account) below.

`www.legesher.io` itself has no accounts and takes no payments. Accounts live at `app.legesher.io`, payments are handled by Polar, and the Translation API runs at `api.legesher.io`. What each one holds is described in [Our Products](#our-products) and [Where Your Data Lives](#where-your-data-lives).

### Information Collected Automatically

When you visit our website, our hosting provider and analytics services may automatically collect:

## Cookies and Tracking Technologies {#cookies-and-tracking}

### We do not set cookies on www.legesher.io

**We set zero cookies on your browser, and we do not write to `localStorage`, `sessionStorage`, or any other client-side storage.** Our analytics (<a href="https://vercel.com/docs/analytics/privacy-policy" rel="noopener">Vercel Web Analytics</a> and <a href="https://posthog.com/privacy" rel="noopener">PostHog</a>, neither of which stores anything on your device) and hosting stack are cookieless, and we do not use cookies for advertising, cross-site tracking, or behavioural profiling. Our newsletter form submits to our own server-side endpoint and never loads third-party scripts in your browser, so <a href="https://buttondown.com" rel="noopener">Buttondown</a> cannot set cookies through our site either.

The account app at `app.legesher.io` is different: it sets the cookies needed to keep you signed in and to protect the sign-in flow. It sets no advertising or cross-site tracking cookies. Its usage events (see [Legesher Account](#legesher-account)) are sent from our servers, so they put nothing on your device.

These sign-in cookies are set by Clerk, our sign-in provider, on our behalf. They are strictly necessary for signing in and keeping your account secure, so we don't ask for consent to use them. They hold no advertising or tracking data. The account app also keeps a few Clerk settings in your browser's local storage, used for sign-in and to protect accounts from automated abuse.

| Name | Type | Set by | Purpose | Expires |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `__session`, `__session_WAgH8YD0` | Cookie | Clerk | Proves you are signed in | Up to 1 year |
| `__client_uat`, `__client_uat_WAgH8YD0` | Cookie | Clerk | Keeps your signed-in state in step across Legesher sites | Up to about 13 months |
| `clerk_active_context` | Cookie | Clerk | Remembers which session is active | When you close the browser |
| `__clerk_environment` | Local storage | Clerk | Caches sign-in settings | Until cleared |
| `__clerk_protect_pid`, `__clerk_protect_st:clerk.legesher.io` | Local storage | Clerk | Protects sign-in from bots and abuse | Until cleared |

Because we set no cookies and use no cookie-based analytics, we do not display a cookie consent banner: none is required under GDPR or the ePrivacy Directive.

### Legesher Account (app.legesher.io) {#legesher-account}

A Legesher account is where you manage your API keys, your plan and the repositories you connect. You sign in with GitHub or with an email address through <a href="https://clerk.com/legal/privacy" rel="noopener">Clerk</a>, which holds your email address, your sign-in credentials and the GitHub link.

Our own account record holds an account identifier, your GitHub user id and username when you sign in with GitHub, the home region recorded for your account, the version of the Terms you accepted, and the time and wording of any newsletter consent you gave. It never holds a password or an API key. The record is stored on Railway, beside the Translation API.

**Your languages.** If you choose them, at sign-up or in Settings, your account record also holds the languages you know, the languages you want to code in, and any founding language you pick. They are deleted with your account.

**Account emails and the newsletter box.** The welcome message and purchase confirmations are sent through Resend. If you tick the newsletter box at sign-up, your email address is added to our newsletter list at Buttondown and to our relationship records at Attio. Nothing else about your account goes to either.

**Usage events.** To understand how people use the account, the app records a few events in PostHog: starting sign-up, creating your account, creating or revoking an API key, opening checkout and confirming a plan. Starting sign-up is counted under a random identifier, not your account. The others are tied to your Legesher account identifier, which stays the same if you change your username or email address. It is never tied to your email address, your name or your GitHub username. The events are sent from our servers, so nothing is stored on your device, and your IP address is not kept. When your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, no event is sent.

**Deleting your account.** Use Delete account on the Settings page of your account once any paid subscription on it has ended, or email [privacy@legesher.com](mailto:privacy@legesher.com). Deleting your account removes your account record, your sign-in details, your languages and your repository connections, and ends your API keys at once. Translation API usage records are kept without any link to your account, problem reports you sent stay with Linear without your name or email address, and we keep a one-way hash of the deleted account's identifier as a record that it was deleted. Settled invoices stay with Polar for as long as accounting rules require. To also remove the usage events tied to your account, ask us at [privacy@legesher.com](mailto:privacy@legesher.com) and we will delete them.

**Public GitHub activity, read for credit.** To credit contributions, we read public activity on Legesher's own GitHub repositories (merged pull requests and reviews) by the GitHub account you sign in with. Nothing outside those repositories is read. To stop this, disconnect GitHub from your account or email [privacy@legesher.com](mailto:privacy@legesher.com).

**The Legesher GitHub App.** To let Legesher read a repository or write maps to it, you, or an administrator of your organisation, install the Legesher GitHub App on the repositories you choose, through GitHub's own consent screen, at one of two levels:

- **Read** lets Legesher read the repository's metadata and contents, to verify maps and report on legibility.
- **Write** adds the ability to write contents and open pull requests, so Legesher can add or update maps under `.legesher/`.

We store which repositories you connected, at which level, and which maps were written to them, so you can see and remove them from your account. The App's access tokens are short-lived and are not stored. Code read through the App is your content: it is processed to produce the map or the report and handled under the Translation API rules below. Uninstalling the App, or removing a repository from it, ends our access.

### Legesher Translation API (api.legesher.io) {#translation-api}

The Translation API translates code between languages for the command line, Legesher Bridge and the tools that connect to it. The source you send is processed in memory and returned. It is not written to logs and not used to train models, and apart from the names described under Enhanced mode it is not kept after the request. What remains is a pseudonymous usage record for each request: who made it (your account or key or, without an account, a salted hash of your network address), when, with which tool and plan, in which languages, how large it was and whether it succeeded. If you name a repository, the record holds a salted hash of that name, not the name itself. It never holds your code. Like any online service, the API also keeps service logs of requests, not their content.

The API and its database run on <a href="https://railway.com/legal/privacy" rel="noopener">Railway</a>. The database holds a hash of each API key, your subscription state and the usage records. It holds no email address and no code.

**Enhanced mode.** On plans that include it, you can ask for enhanced mode in a request. The comments, docstrings, text strings and names from your code are then also sent to a language model provider for the model pass, and the translation comes back to you. The structure of your code is not sent, and what is sent is not used to train models. So that a name is translated the same way from one request to the next, when you identify a repository the service keeps the names from it, together with their translations. They are kept for your account only and are not shared with other customers. Enhanced mode is off unless you ask for it.

**Payments.** Plans are sold through <a href="https://polar.sh/legal/privacy" rel="noopener">Polar</a>, our merchant of record. Polar collects your name, email address, billing address and payment details, and handles taxes, invoices and refunds. We receive your email address, the plan you bought and its status. We never see your card details.

### Contributed translations

If you contribute a translation, the word you offer and a record that it was endorsed become part of the Legesher language corpus, a research dataset. **Your name does not.** You appear only as an opaque identifier derived from your account name using a secret key, so the dataset contains no names, handles, or email addresses. The mapping back to you is held privately and is never included in any published copy.

The corpus is published on Hugging Face under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence (CC BY 4.0). Please contribute on the understanding that the word you offer will be readable by anyone.

Credit is opt-in: you are pseudonymous by default, and your contribution counts the same either way. You may ask us at any time to erase the data that identifies you. The translations you offered remain in the dataset as pseudonymous records, because other contributors' work depends on them, but nothing connects them to you.

Two limits on that erasure, so you can weigh them before contributing. If you submitted through a public pull request or issue, that submission sits in a public repository under your own account, and we cannot unpublish something you published yourself. And where your details were recorded in our own repository, removing them is a change going forward; earlier revision history is cleared separately rather than on request. Ask us where that stands and we will tell you.

To be credited, change how you are credited, withdraw, or read the full contributor notice, email [privacy@legesher.com](mailto:privacy@legesher.com). The notice ships with the project repository and will be linked here directly once that repository is public.

Third-party community platforms we link to (GitHub, Slack, LinkedIn, Instagram) are governed by their own privacy policies, not this one.
