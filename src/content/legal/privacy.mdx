## Information We Collect {#information-we-collect}

### Information You Provide to Us

We collect information you voluntarily provide, including:

- **Newsletter Subscriptions:** first name and email address, submitted through our newsletter form and processed by <a href="https://buttondown.com" rel="noopener">Buttondown</a>. When you submit the form, your IP address is also forwarded (server-side, not from your browser) to Buttondown as part of the subscription payload, which they may use for their own spam prevention and audit logs. If you reached the page through a campaign link, the `utm_source` and `utm_campaign` values from that link's address are recorded with your subscription so we can tell which of our announcements reached you. These are read from the address of the page you subscribe from and are never stored on your device; when the address carries no such values, nothing is recorded.
- **Survey Responses:** feedback provided through optional surveys we link to. Our surveys are hosted on Google Forms; responses are collected by Google on our behalf under their privacy terms.
- **Email Correspondence:** any information you include when emailing us directly (for example, at [hello@legesher.com](mailto:hello@legesher.com) or [privacy@legesher.com](mailto:privacy@legesher.com)).

Our own account record holds the identifiers and choices described under [Legesher Account](#legesher-account) below.

`www.legesher.io` itself has no accounts and takes no payments. Accounts live at `app.legesher.io`, payments are handled by Polar, and the Translation API runs at `api.legesher.io`. What each one holds, and where, is described in [Our Products](#our-products) and [Where Your Data Lives](#where-your-data-lives).

## Cookies and Tracking Technologies {#cookies-and-tracking}

### We do not set cookies

**We set zero cookies on your browser, and we do not write to `localStorage`, `sessionStorage`, or any other client-side storage.** Our analytics (<a href="https://vercel.com/docs/analytics/privacy-policy" rel="noopener">Vercel Web Analytics</a>) and hosting stack are cookieless, and we do not use cookies for advertising, cross-site tracking, or behavioural profiling. Our newsletter form submits to our own server-side endpoint and never loads third-party scripts in your browser, so <a href="https://buttondown.com" rel="noopener">Buttondown</a> cannot set cookies through our site either.

The account app at `app.legesher.io` is different: it sets the cookies needed to keep you signed in and to protect the sign-in flow. It sets no advertising or cross-site tracking cookies. The sign-in cookie is set for the legesher.io domain as a whole, so our documentation site can tell whether you are signed in without running sign-in code of its own.

Because we set no cookies and use no cookie-based analytics, we do not display a cookie consent banner — none is required under GDPR or the ePrivacy Directive.

### Fonts {#fonts}

The website loads the Noto Sans font family from Google Fonts (`fonts.googleapis.com`). Google may receive your IP address when your browser requests these font files. We are in the process of self-hosting Noto Sans to remove this third-party dependency; until that change ships, you can block Google Fonts at the browser level without affecting site functionality.

### Your Cookie Choices

You can control cookies and client-side storage through:

- **Browser settings:** most browsers allow you to refuse or delete cookies and clear local storage.
- **Do Not Track:** we honour Do Not Track browser signals where technically feasible. In practice, our analytics stack does not profile visitors regardless.

### Legesher Account (app.legesher.io) {#legesher-account}

A Legesher account is where you manage your API keys, your plan and the repositories you connect. You sign in with GitHub or with an email address through <a href="https://clerk.com/legal/privacy" rel="noopener">Clerk</a>, which holds your email address, your sign-in credentials and the GitHub link, in the United States.

Our own account record holds an account identifier, your GitHub user id and username when you sign in with GitHub, the home region recorded for your account, the version of the Terms you accepted, and the time and wording of any newsletter consent you gave. It never holds a password or an API key. The record is stored in the United States (Railway, US West), beside the Translation API.

**Account emails and the newsletter box.** The welcome message and purchase confirmations are sent through Resend. If you tick the newsletter box at sign-up, your email address is added to our newsletter list at Buttondown and to our relationship records at Attio. Nothing else about your account goes to either.

**Public GitHub activity, read for credit.** To credit contributions, we read public activity on Legesher's own GitHub repositories (merged pull requests and reviews) by the GitHub account you sign in with. Nothing outside those repositories is read. To stop this, disconnect GitHub from your account or email [privacy@legesher.com](mailto:privacy@legesher.com).

**The Legesher GitHub App.** To let Legesher read a repository or write maps to it, you, or an administrator of your organisation, install the Legesher GitHub App on the repositories you choose, through GitHub's own consent screen, at one of two levels:

- **Read** lets Legesher read the repository's metadata and contents, to verify maps and report on legibility.
- **Write** adds the ability to write contents and open pull requests, so Legesher can add or update maps under `.legesher/`.

We store which repositories you connected, at which level, and which maps were written to them, so you can see and remove them from your account. The App's access tokens are short-lived and are not stored. Code read through the App is your content: it is processed to produce the map or the report and handled under the Translation API rules below. Uninstalling the App, or removing a repository from it, ends our access.

### Legesher Translation API (api.legesher.io) {#translation-api}

The Translation API translates code between languages for the command line, Legesher Bridge and the tools that connect to it. The source you send is processed in memory and returned. It is not written to logs, not kept after the request, and not used to train models. What remains is a pseudonymous usage record: sizes, language codes and an outcome code, never your code.

The API and its database run on <a href="https://railway.com/legal/privacy" rel="noopener">Railway</a> in the United States (US West). The database holds a hash of each API key, your subscription state and the usage records. It holds no email address and no code.

**Enhanced mode.** When enhanced mode is enabled for your plan, the comments, docstrings, text strings and names from your code are also sent to <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy" rel="noopener">Anthropic</a>, in the United States, for the model pass. The structure of your code is not sent. Enhanced mode is off unless your plan includes it.

**Payments.** Plans are sold through <a href="https://polar.sh/legal/privacy" rel="noopener">Polar</a>, our merchant of record, in the United States. Polar collects your name, email address, billing address and payment details, and handles taxes, invoices and refunds. We receive your email address, the plan you bought and its status. We never see your card details.

### Contributed translations

If you contribute a translation, the word you offer and a record that it was endorsed become part of the Legesher language corpus, a research dataset. **Your name does not.** You appear only as an opaque identifier derived from your account name using a secret key, so the dataset contains no names, handles, or email addresses. The mapping back to you is held privately and is never included in any published copy.

The corpus is currently distributed privately, with access granted on request, and is intended to become openly available under a Creative Commons Attribution licence. Please contribute on the understanding that the word you offer will eventually be readable by anyone.

Credit is opt-in: you are anonymous by default, and your contribution counts the same either way. You may ask us at any time to erase the data that identifies you. The translations you offered remain in the dataset as anonymous records, because other contributors' work depends on them, but nothing connects them to you.

Two limits on that erasure, so you can weigh them before contributing. If you submitted through a public pull request or issue, that submission sits in a public repository under your own account, and we cannot unpublish something you published yourself. And where your details were recorded in our own repository, removing them is a change going forward; earlier revision history is cleared separately rather than on request. Ask us where that stands and we will tell you.

To be credited, change how you are credited, withdraw, or read the full contributor notice, email [privacy@legesher.com](mailto:privacy@legesher.com). The notice ships with the project repository and will be linked here directly once that repository is public.

Third-party community platforms we link to (GitHub, Slack, LinkedIn, Instagram) are governed by their own privacy policies, not this one.

Similar rights may apply under your state's privacy laws. Contact us to exercise your rights.

### European Economic Area (EEA) and UK Residents

If you are located in the EEA or UK, you have rights under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) or UK GDPR:

- **Access:** request a copy of your personal data
- **Rectification:** request correction of inaccurate data
- **Erasure:** request deletion (the “right to be forgotten”)
- **Restriction:** request limitation of processing
- **Portability:** receive your data in a portable format
- **Object** to certain processing activities
- **Withdraw consent** at any time
- **Lodge a complaint** with a supervisory authority — for EEA residents, your local Data Protection Authority; for UK residents, the <a href="https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint/" rel="noopener">Information Commissioner's Office</a>

**Legal basis for processing (EEA/UK):**

- Consent (e.g., newsletter subscriptions)
- Legitimate interests (e.g., aggregated analytics, security)
- Contract performance (e.g., responding to inquiries, providing your account and the Translation API)
- Legal obligation (e.g., compliance with laws)
